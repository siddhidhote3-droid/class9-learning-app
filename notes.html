🔬 SCIENCE (Ch 1–18)

1. Motion

  • Motion is change in position with time.
  • Distance is scalar; displacement is vector.
  • Speed = distance/time.
  • Acceleration is rate of change of velocity.

2. Laws of Motion

  • First law: State of rest or motion remains unchanged.
  • Second law: Force = mass × acceleration.
  • Third law: Action and reaction are equal and opposite.

3. Gravitation

  • Every object attracts another object.
  • Weight = mass × gravity.
  • Free fall occurs due to gravity.

4. Work and Energy

  • Work is done when force causes displacement.
  • Kinetic energy depends on motion.
  • Potential energy depends on position.

5. Sound

  • Sound is produced by vibration.
  • Amplitude controls loudness.
  • Frequency controls pitch.

6. Matter in Our Surroundings

  • Matter has mass and occupies space.
  • Three states: solid, liquid, gas.
  • Change of state occurs due to temperature.

7. Is Matter Around Us Pure

  • Pure substance has fixed composition.
  • Mixture contains two or more substances.
  • Solutions, colloids and suspensions are mixtures.

8. Atoms and Molecules

  • Atoms are building blocks of matter.
  • Law of conservation of mass.
  • Mole relates mass and number of particles.

9. Structure of the Atom

  • Atom has nucleus and electrons.
  • Protons are positive, electrons negative.
  • Bohr model explains electron shells.

10. Cell – Structure and Function

  • Cell is basic unit of life.
  • Nucleus controls activities.
  • Mitochondria is powerhouse.

11. Tissues

  • Tissues are group of similar cells.
  • Plant tissues: meristematic, permanent.
  • Animal tissues: epithelial, muscular, nervous.

12. Diversity in Living Organisms

  • Living organisms are classified into groups.
  • Classification helps in study.
  • Based on similarities and differences.

13. Why Do We Fall Ill

  • Diseases affect body function.
  • Causes: infection, poor nutrition.
  • Prevention is better than cure.

14. Natural Resources

  • Resources are gifts of nature.
  • Air, water, soil are important.
  • Conservation is necessary.

15. Improvement in Food Resources

  • Better crops increase yield.
  • Manure and fertilizers improve soil.
  • Animal husbandry supports food supply.

16. Environment

  • Environment includes biotic and abiotic factors.
  • Ecosystem is interaction of organisms.
  • Balance is necessary.

17. Natural Disasters

  • Earthquakes, floods, cyclones are disasters.
  • They cause loss of life and property.
  • Preparedness reduces damage.

18. Pollution

  • Pollution makes environment harmful.
  • Air, water and soil pollution.
  • Control is necessary for health.

📐 MATHEMATICS (ALL)

1. Number Systems

  • Natural, whole, integers, rational and irrational numbers.
  • Real numbers include rational and irrational.

2. Polynomials

  • Expression with variables and coefficients.
  • Degree depends on highest power.

3. Linear Equations in Two Variables

  • Equation of form ax + by + c = 0.
  • Graph is a straight line.

4. Coordinate Geometry

  • Plane divided into four quadrants.
  • Point represented as (x, y).

5. Euclid’s Geometry

  • Based on axioms and postulates.
  • Foundation of geometry.

6. Lines and Angles

  • Angles formed by intersecting lines.
  • Parallel lines have special angle relations.

7. Triangles

  • Triangles have three sides.
  • Congruency rules: SSS, SAS, ASA.

8. Quadrilaterals

  • Four sided figures.
  • Parallelogram properties.

9. Areas of Parallelograms and Triangles

  • Area depends on base and height.
  • Same base and height → equal area.

10. Circles

  • Set of points at equal distance from center.
  • Radius and diameter important.

11. Constructions

  • Geometrical drawing using compass.
  • Accuracy is important.

12. Heron’s Formula

  • Used to find area of triangle.
  • Needs all three sides.

13. Surface Areas and Volumes

  • 3D shapes like cube, cylinder.
  • Formulas used for calculation.

14. Statistics

  • Collection of data.
  • Mean, median and mode.

15. Probability

  • Chance of an event.
  • Probability = favourable / total outcomes.
